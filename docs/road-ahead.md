# The Beautiful Game — The Road Ahead

This document explains the **game TBG is building beyond the current controlled alpha**.

It is intentionally separate from the Alpha Rulebook.

The Alpha Rulebook answers: **what can a manager do now?**

This document answers: **what is the constitutional destination?**

The constitutions remain authoritative for the detailed design. This page is a public, player-friendly overview, not a promise that every system below will arrive in a particular order or release.

---

## The Idea

The long-term goal is not simply to simulate football matches.

TBG is intended to simulate a **football world** in which clubs, players and managers have histories, incentives and consequences that persist over many seasons.

The deeper systems are designed around one principle:

**football knowledge and judgement should matter more than clicking fastest, exploiting hidden formulas or inheriting the richest owner.**

---

## 1. Players Who Make Career Decisions

The current alpha transfer market is primarily an agreement between managers and clubs.

The constitutional destination adds the player himself to the decision.

Players will weigh factors such as:

- wages and contract security;
- likely playing time;
- club strength and reputation;
- chances of promotion and trophies;
- development opportunities;
- manager reputation;
- personal ambition and career stage;
- geographical preferences.

Different players will value those things differently.

That means the highest wage or biggest club will not automatically win every recruitment battle.

---

## 2. Recruitment Windows Instead of Click Races

When several clubs compete for an available player, the design supports **Recruitment Windows**.

Managers get a fair opportunity to make their case before the player decides between the available offers.

The intention is to reward judgement and squad-building strategy rather than whoever happens to be online first.

---

## 3. Agents, Contracts and Promises

The contract system is intended to become much richer.

Players and agents can care about:

- wages;
- contract length;
- bonuses;
- release clauses;
- playing status;
- development pathways;
- promotion or trophy ambitions.

Managers may be able to make promises such as first-team football or a development pathway.

Keeping promises should strengthen a manager's reputation. Breaking them should have consequences.

---

## 4. Scouting as Knowledge, Not Hidden Players

Every manager should ultimately be able to discover the same real footballers.

The deeper scouting game is about **how well you understand them**.

Managers will build expertise in a limited number of **Knowledge Regions**. Maintaining knowledge over time improves the quality and confidence of information available there.

Changing focus should carry an opportunity cost because accumulated regional expertise matters.

The aim is to reproduce one of football management's great advantages: knowing a market better than your rivals.

---

## 5. Young Players and Development

Young real-world footballers will continue entering the world as they emerge in real football.

The deeper youth system is designed to make development history matter.

Clubs that genuinely identify, play and develop young players should build a reputation for doing so.

That can make them more attractive to future prospects and may generate **Development Levy** compensation when qualifying players move later in their careers.

A smaller club with a credible pathway should therefore sometimes beat a giant club for a young player's signature.

---

## 6. Clubs With Expectations

The future board system gives every managerial job context.

A club will have an **Expected Finish** and a public **Contract Objective** suited to its level.

Examples include:

- avoid relegation;
- consolidate;
- finish in the top half;
- win promotion;
- challenge for the title;
- win a specified trophy.

Success will be judged relative to expectation rather than simply by league position in isolation.

That makes taking a modest club from 18th to 9th potentially more impressive than finishing 2nd with a club expected to win the league.

---

## 7. Board Confidence and the Sack Race

Managers will not have jobs for life.

The constitutional career system includes **Board Confidence**, allowing persistent serious underperformance to put a manager's job at risk.

The intended exit routes include:

- relegation;
- sustained lost confidence;
- abandonment;
- voluntary departure for another job.

The system is designed to be published and deterministic rather than dependent on administrator whim.

---

## 8. Earned Board Backing

Clubs differ naturally in size and economic power, but boards are intended to operate by the same rules.

Managers who outperform expectations can earn additional **Board Backing**.

This creates a way for excellent management at a smaller club to produce resources and momentum without introducing arbitrary rich owners.

---

## 9. A Deeper Football Economy

The controlled alpha starts with cash, wage bills, wage budgets and affordability.

The constitutional economy goes much further, including:

- club revenue;
- expected wage curves;
- scouting investment;
- transfer budgets;
- market-value transfer controls;
- financial sustainability rules;
- deterministic sanctions for serious breaches.

The proposed sanction ladder includes transfer restrictions, wage restrictions and ultimately points deductions.

The intention is to create meaningful financial strategy without allowing runaway inflation or owner wealth to overwhelm football judgement.

---

## 10. Transfer Rumours and an Information Game

Not every piece of transfer activity should immediately become public.

The future information system distinguishes private interest from activity serious enough to generate rumours.

Repeated or substantial pursuit of a player may become news, potentially affecting the player's situation without exposing another club's private bid details.

That creates a strategic information layer around recruitment rather than turning the transfer market into a completely transparent auction board.

---

## 11. A Living Football Press

World Feed v0 is the beginning of a shared social layer.

The constitutional destination includes a richer football-media world:

- match reports;
- league roundups;
- transfer stories;
- records and milestones;
- pre-match and post-match questions;
- manager press stances;
- journalists and recurring narratives.

Managers should normally interact through meaningful choices rather than being required to write essays twice a week.

Press behaviour may influence supporters, boards or reputation, but it should never directly rig a football result.

---

## 12. Manager Reputation and Career Identity

Managers will build careers that persist beyond one club.

Their records can eventually reflect things such as:

- trophies;
- promotions;
- performance against expectations;
- youth development;
- financial stewardship;
- promises kept;
- tenure and stability.

The game is designed to let manager identities **emerge from behaviour**.

You do not click a button saying *Youth Developer* or *Promotion Specialist*.

You become one by repeatedly doing it.

---

## 13. Professionalism and Participation

TBG should reward reliable participation without demanding constant online presence.

The constitutional system includes a public **Professionalism** record and a participation framework covering reasonable response times, Away Mode and a staged process for persistent abandonment.

The intended sequence is:

**Warning → Caretaker → Removal**

The caretaker exists to keep the shared world functioning, not to punish legitimate absence.

---

## 14. Promotion Playoffs

The canonical competition structure is now intended to use:

- three automatic promotion places;
- a four-team playoff involving 4th–7th;
- one additional promotion place for the playoff winner;
- four automatic relegation places from the division above.

The current engine does not yet implement this playoff stage. It is therefore a concrete example of governance describing the intended game ahead of implementation.

---

## 15. Cups and a Broader Competition Calendar

The wider constitutional world includes domestic cup competitions and leaves room for future continental or international competitions.

These give managers more ways to build careers and create history than league position alone.

The controlled multiplayer alpha deliberately prioritises the shared league/market/world spine first.

---

## 16. Loans and More Complex Deals

The transfer architecture is intended to expand beyond straight purchases.

Planned transaction breadth includes:

- first-class loans;
- automatic loan returns;
- multi-player exchanges;
- three-or-more-club transactions.

These systems must preserve TBG's core transaction rule: a deal either completes correctly as a whole or leaves the canonical world unchanged.

---

## 17. What Will Not Change

As TBG becomes deeper, several principles should remain stable:

- one persistent shared world;
- public rules;
- real-world evidence as the basis of player quality;
- in-world ownership independent of real-world transfers;
- no admin-assigned secret player boosts;
- no click-race advantage for important recruitment decisions;
- no unequal sugar-daddy owners;
- no direct press-conference bonus to match results;
- no rewriting player Ability because of one TBG performance;
- no sacrificing transaction safety or world integrity for feature breadth.

---

## The Destination

The long-term TBG manager should have to think about much more than the next formation.

Who fits the squad?

Who will actually sign?

Which markets do you understand?

Which young players deserve minutes?

What promises can you safely make?

How much can the club afford?

How patient is the board?

What sort of manager are you becoming?

And, eventually, what will the history of this one shared football world say you achieved?
